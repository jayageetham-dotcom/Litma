Every work in Litma has a writing type (സാഹിത്യരൂപം). It decides the page layout, the fonts and the styles on the toolbar, so a screenplay looks like a screenplay and a poem like a poem. These guides show what each one gives you.

## Choosing a writing type {#choose-type}

When you choose **New work**, Litma shows ten writing types with a preview of their first page. **Note** comes first, for anything that needs no template:

| Writing type | Good for |
| --- | --- |
| കുറിപ്പ് · Note | Anything else: a plain page with no template |
| നോവൽ · Novel | Long fiction in chapters |
| ചെറുകഥ · Short story | A story in one piece |
| കവിത · Poem | Verse, with metre marks when you want them |
| തിരക്കഥ · Screenplay | Film scripts: scenes, action and dialogue |
| ഫീച്ചർ · Feature writing | Magazine and newspaper features |
| ലേഖനം · Essay / article | Essays and articles with footnotes |
| നാടകം · Drama / play | Plays: cast list, acts, scenes and stage directions |
| ഗാനം · Song lyrics | Songs with പല്ലവി and അനുപല്ലവി |
| ജേണൽ · Journal | Dated entries, travelogues and memoir |

> **Good to know:** The writing type can't be changed after you start. If you picked the wrong one, start a new work of the right type and paste your text in.

## Novels and short stories {#novel}

![A novel in the editor](shots/editor.webp)

- The template gives a title, the author line (രചന), a മേൽക്കുറിപ്പ് (epigraph) and the first chapter.
- Use **Chapter** from the style menu for each new chapter. Chapters appear in the outline, and you can have them numbered (**Page & type → Number the chapters**).
- The first letter after a chapter is drawn larger, like in printed books.
- **Scene break** (⁂) separates scenes inside a chapter.
- Paragraphs are indented like a printed book, except the first one after a chapter or a break. An empty line works as a quiet section break.

## Poems and song lyrics {#poem}

![A poem with metre marks](shots/poem-metre.webp "Metre marks show the akshara count and the laghu and guru of each line.")

- Each line is its own line; press <kbd>Enter</kbd> for the next. An empty line starts a new stanza.
- Poem lines can be centred: **Page & type → Lines → Centred**.
- Song lyrics have പല്ലവി, അനുപല്ലവി and ചരണം labels. Press <kbd>Enter</kbd> on a label to move to its first line.

### Metre marks (വൃത്തം)

Choose **വൃത്തം** on the toolbar, or press <kbd>Ctrl</kbd> + <kbd>Alt</kbd> + <kbd>V</kbd>. Litma shows how many aksharas each line has and marks every syllable as laghu (short) or guru (long), so you can check the metre as you write. The marks never print. Choose it again to hide them.

For words that rhyme, with the same laghu and guru, use the [rhyme finder](#rhymes). To find out which metre a stanza is in, use the [metre checker](#metre).

## Check the metre (വൃത്തം) {#metre}

In a poem or song lyrics, the metre checker names the metre of the stanza you are writing and shows which syllables don't fit it yet.

![The metre checker beside a poem](shots/metre.webp "The stanza's metre, its rule, and each line in syllables with laghu (∪) and guru (–).")

1. Inside a poem or lyrics, choose **Metre (വൃത്തം)** on the right-hand rail. On a phone, choose **More**, then **Metre**; in the Litma phone app, the tools button, then **Metre**.
2. Put the cursor anywhere in a stanza. The checker reads that stanza; on an empty line it reads the stanza above.
3. Read the box at the top: the metre's name, its rule (the ganas, or the aksharas in each line), and how well the stanza fits.
4. Each line is shown in syllables, grouped by gana, with ∪ for laghu and – for guru. A syllable marked in amber needs the other weight; a line with the wrong number of aksharas says how many it needs. Tap a syllable to find it in your text.

What the checker can say:

- **Every line fits:** a Sanskrit metre (സമവൃത്തം and the like: വസന്തതിലകം, മന്ദാക്രാന്ത, ശാർദ്ദൂലവിക്രീഡിതം, അനുഷ്ടുപ്പ്, ഉപജാതി and others) whose laghu–guru pattern every line follows. The last syllable of a line may be either.
- **Likely:** a Malayalam metre (ഭാഷാവൃത്തം: കേക, കാകളി, മഞ്ജരി, നതോന്നത, അന്നനട, തരംഗിണി and others). These are sung, so the checker reads them from the aksharas in each pair of lines and the shape of the ganas.
- **Close:** a few syllables, or one line, are off. They are marked.
- **No common metre found:** free verse needs none. The checker never marks free verse as wrong.

> **Good to know:** A ് at the end of a word (അത്, പാട്ട്) can be read as a short syllable or not counted at all, as poets do both. The checker takes whichever fits the metre and shows an uncounted one faded.

The panel also has a switch for the [metre marks](#poem) beside each line.

## Screenplays {#screenplay}

![A screenplay](shots/screenplay.webp)

A screenplay is written in blocks. The toolbar shows them as buttons: **Scene**, **Time**, **Cast**, **Action**, **Dialogue** and **Transition**.

1. Start a scene with **Scene**. Open **Scene…** on the toolbar to pick **Int.** or **Ext.** and type the place.
2. Add the **Time of day** (പകൽ, രാത്രി…) and the **Cast** in the scene. **Fill the cast list** in **Scene…** adds everyone who speaks in that scene.
3. Write what we see as **Action**.
4. For a line of dialogue, choose **Dialogue**, type the character's name, press <kbd>Enter</kbd> and type what they say.

Scenes are numbered for you (രംഗം 1 or Scene 1, chosen in **Page & type**) and listed in the outline.

## Plays {#drama}

![A play with a cast list](shots/drama.webp)

- **Cast list:** each entry has a name and a short description. The **Cast list** button adds your Story World characters (add them first in Story World → Characters).
- **Act** and **Scene** blocks are numbered for you and shown in both languages in the menus (അങ്കം · Act). Scene numbers start again in each act.
- **Stage direction** is set in italics between brackets.
- Dialogue works like in a screenplay: the name, then <kbd>Enter</kbd>, then the speech.

## Features and essays {#feature}

![A feature with a footnote](shots/feature.webp)

- Features have a kicker (the small line above the title), a standfirst (the short lead under the title), a byline and pull quotes.
- **Footnotes:** put the cursor where the note belongs, choose **Footnote** on the toolbar, type the note and choose **Add footnote**. A small number appears in the text and the notes are listed at the end of the work, in the PDF and in the Word file. Click a number to edit or remove its note.

## Journal {#diary}

![A journal with dated entries](shots/diary.webp)

Choose **New entry** on the toolbar to add today's date at the end and start writing under it. The outline lists every date, so you can jump back to any day.

Journals live in their own **Journals** tab on the desk, not among your works. Each one is named after the day you started it, until you give it a title of your own. See [Journals](#journals).

## Journals {#journals}

Your journals have their own tab on the desk, newest first, with a line for each month. They stay private: a journal can't be shared by link or added to a book.

![The Journals tab on the desk](shots/journals.webp)

### Start a journal

1. On the desk choose the **Journals** tab.
2. Choose **New journal**. It opens straight away, named after today's date, for example 2026 നവംബർ 28, ശനിയാഴ്ച.
3. Write. Give it a title of your own at the top if you like.

A new journal you leave without writing anything is removed, like any other empty work.

### The journal cards

- The small calendar on each card shows the day you started the journal.
- A journal still named after its date shows its first line of writing as the title, in bold.
- **Edited** and a date appear at the bottom when you last changed it on another day.
- **Search journals** (when you have more than three) finds words in titles and text, in Malayalam or Manglish.

> **Phone:** Tap **New journal** at the bottom right. Slide the tabs sideways if Journals is out of view.

### Move between journals

In a journal, the arrows on each side of the title open the journal you started before (‹) and after (›) it. Your writing is saved first. Under the title you see when the journal was started and last edited. At your first or latest journal that arrow is greyed out.

### Export your journals {#export-journals}

1. In a journal choose **Export** at the top (phone: **More → Export journals**), or choose **Export** on the Journals tab.
2. Pick **This journal** or **All journals**. For all journals, pick the dates they were started between.
3. Pick **PDF** or **Word (.docx)** and choose **Export**.

All journals make one file: oldest first, with a contents page, and each journal starting on a new page. A PDF opens in the print view: choose **Save as PDF** there.

> **Good to know:** Offline, journals you have never opened on this device can't go into the file yet. Litma tells you how many, and makes the file from the rest.
